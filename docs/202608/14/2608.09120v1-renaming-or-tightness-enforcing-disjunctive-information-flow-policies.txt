Abstract —A disjunctive policy allows a value to depend on at most one of two secrets and never on both: an analyst may consult one client’s file or the other’s, a share of a split secret may be released but not its sibling. Such policies are not lattice-shaped, and Hunt and Sands introduced the quantale of information to give them a semantics, leaving the enforcement layer open. We build the flow-sensitive type system family that the quantale calls for, and show that the object which makes such families useful, the universal type object from which every member specialises, splits in two, with a consequence for enforcement. Over the free commutative quantale on the program variables the whole mechanism survives for every policy: monotone renaming, canonical derivations, principal typings, internal completeness. Over the free object with idempotent generators the certified bound is strictly more precise and still sound, because it records that two reads of one source honour one disjunct. The gap cannot be closed from inside the independent-attribute family: no mechanism of that shape whose labelling maps support monotone renaming certifies a bound more precise than the first, and for principal typings under generator-exact homomorphic specialisation the two coincide. Under the ethical-wall and secret-sharing labels the second read of a disjunctive source therefore drives every such certificate to no guarantee , and programs that satisfy the policy are rejected. The literal transcription of the lattice-era object is no escape either: it is a further quotient that loses branch disjunction. Precision is recovered by deferring specialisation to the judgement level, and the resulting read-out map is the least sound join-preserving one. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

A consultancy holds the files of two competing clients. Its ethical wall, the Chinese Wall of Brewer and Nash [4], says that no report may depend on both. A key-management service splits a secret into two shares [15] and releases at most one. A clinical dashboard may show a patient’s diagnosis or their identity, never the pair. What these policies forbid is not a release but a combination of otherwise permissible releases, and no lattice of security levels expresses that: in a lattice the join of two admissible dependencies is admissible again. Hunt and Sands answered this with the quantale of informa-tion [2], which keeps the lattice of information [5] as a special case and adds a second combination operator. Disjunction remains the join, and conjunction becomes a tensor ⊗ that is 

not idempotent, so that combining a disjunctive dependency with itself may reveal strictly more than the dependency alone. That is exactly what an ethical wall is about. Lattice-based enforcement is a mature line, from certification through security type systems to flow-sensitive analyses [7], [8], [9], [1]. What has been missing for the quantale is enforcement. [2] closes by naming the step: adapt the flow-sensitive dependency analysis of [1] so that it becomes the language of types over the quantale. Disjunctive policies have since been enforced for a database query language against a purpose-built condition [3], but the general question of what a type system can certify about a disjunctive policy, and of what it must fail to certify, has not been asked. The analysis they point to is not an arbitrary starting point. Its distinguishing feature is a universal object . Fixing the flow lattice to be the powerset of program variables yields one system that subsumes the whole family: each program has a principal typing there, every typing under every other lattice follows by monotone renaming, and no member of the family certifies more than the target lattice’s own system does. For enforcement this is the difference between analysing a program once and re-analysing it for every policy. The suggestion even names an object: the abstract domain of [2, §V], sets of sets of variables kept irredundant under inclusion, which is the set object U below presented by antichains. This paper carries out the step and reports what follows. The universal object does not break in the passage to a quantale. It splits, the object their roadmap names is one half of the split, and which half one takes decides what can be enforced.

The universal object splits 

The powerset of variables carries two structures at once, and a lattice cannot tell them apart. Read as an ordered monoid under union it is the free commutative idempotent monoid on the variables. With no idempotence imposed, the free commutative monoid has multisets as its elements. Freeness of the associated quantale asks a labelling to extend to a monotone monoid homomorphism, so the idempotent reading forces every label to satisfy σ(v) ⊗ σ(v) = σ(v) while the multiset reading forces nothing beyond affineness. Every labelling into a locale is idempotent, so in the lattice setting the two objects specialise identically and nothing distinguishes them. That is the free locale on the variables, a further quotient of U. It is tight on Example 1, since union is idempotent, but it identifies the join with the tensor and so erases the difference between “this run depended on X1, that run on X2” and “some run depended on X1 ∪ X2”, which is precisely the distinction the quantale exists to make (Corollary 2). Of the three objects, the multiset one loses same-source correlation, the locale one loses branch disjunction, and only U keeps both. 

Renaming or tightness, not both 

Two escapes suggest themselves: write better rules, or build a better universal object. Neither is open. Any sound mechanism that gives each variable an element of M and reads an observation off as a tensor over-counts Example 1, by an argument that names no rule (Proposition 7). And a universal object is precision-neutral: if its specialisation is monotone, lax monoidal and lax unital, which is what transports derivations, then it certifies nothing more precise than re-analysing the program directly in the target quantale (Theorem 7), and for principal typings under generator-exact homomorphic specialisation the two bounds coincide (Proposition 8). Reuse is what such an object buys, and reuse is all it buys: 

On a quantale one may have renaming or tightness, but not both. 

In enforcement terms the consequence is sharp. Call a label 

saturated when σ(v) ⊗ σ(v) = ⊤, which is the shape of the ethical-wall label of Example 1 and of a two-share split, since there the two permitted dependencies jointly determine the secret. Under a saturated label, a single read of a source can be certified against a non-trivial policy and a second read certifies nothing at all (Corollary 1), and there are programs that satisfy the policy, are accepted by the mechanism of Section VIII, and are rejected by every mechanism in the renaming paradigm (Theorem 8). Example 1 is one of them. The loss saturates: by Proposition 5 it is paid in full at the second read and later reads are free. Escaping costs exactly the renaming property. Typing in 

U and applying the labelling to judgements rather than to environments recovers the tight bound (Theorem 9), and that read-out map is the least sound join-preserving one (Theorem 10), so the repair does not merely help, it lands on the floor while the renaming paradigm cannot reach below the ceiling.

II. D ISJUNCTIVE POLICIES AND WHAT A RUN REVEALS 

A. Secrets, observers, policies 

Fix a finite set D of secrets. LoI( D) [5] is the set of equivalence relations on D ordered by P ⊑ Q iff Q ⊆ P ,so larger means more informative, with least element All ,greatest element Id and join P ⊔ Q = P ∩ Q. An observation is a function h on D, and h : P ⇒ Id means P ⊆ ker h: an observer of h cannot separate secrets that P identifies. The quantale of information [2] replaces one relation by a tiling-closed set of them. Writing h : P ⇒ Id for “∃P ∈ P with P ⊆ ker h”, the two operators are disjunctive and conjunctive combination of permissions, and weakening (P ⊑ Q and h : P ⇒ Id imply h : Q ⇒ Id ) is [2, Prop. 1]. The tensor is idempotent only on principal ideals. For the ethical-wall label S = tc {∼ 1, ∼2} of Example 1, S ⊗ S = ⊤.The attacker-facing reading of ⇒ is the knowledge-based one [2, §V]. A knowledge set of an observation h is a nonempty preimage h−1{e}, what an observer of a single output can deduce about the secret, and h : R ⇒ Id holds exactly when every knowledge set of h is tiled by R. A permission is thus an upper bound on each individual deduction, not an average or an expectation, and combining permissions with ⊗ asks what an observer of both outputs can deduce at once.

This is the operational content of a disjunctive permission, and it is what the knowledge-based reading of the join in [2, §V] says: what an observer may deduce on a given run is consistent with one of the disjuncts. Which disjunct is honoured may depend on the secret, hence on the run, and within a run exactly one is honoured. Everything in this paper turns on that sentence: the second read of a source in Example 1 is safe because it happens in the same run as the first. The variable set Var and the set D of secrets are finite, and Val contains an encoding of every finite quotient of D and supports injective pairing, which for finite D is a size assumption only. The paper works at two levels: typing, renaming, algorithmic inference and the universal objects are stated for an arbitrary such M , while every statement that mentions mediators, compliance, soundness or tightness fixes M = QoI( D), whose elements are the sets of equivalence relations that make legality meaningful.

B. Why these rules 

Three points make this the faithful reading rather than one of many. First, the assignment of operators is fixed by the source: [2] defines ⊗ as conjunctive and ∨ as disjunctive combination, so a multi-variable expression, whose value determines all of its operands’ contributions at once, must combine with ⊗,and a branch merge, where a run follows one side or the other, must combine with ∨. Second, the shapes are theirs, not ours: the I F rule is the premise of their Lemma 6 transcribed with environments in place of single functions, and the joint observation of a set of variables is their Lemma 8, the only pairing rule the source provides. Third, [2, Prop. 5] states that the embedding of LoI into QoI sends the lattice join to ⊗, so on environments valued in principal ideals every rule above computes exactly what the corresponding rule of [1] computes over LoI : the tensor is the lattice join, and the guard factors are absorbed because an assigned variable’s type already dominates the context, with t∗ = t by idempotence. On straight-line code F1 therefore reproduces [1] verbatim. The principal ideals are not closed under ∨, so a branch merge leaves them, and there F1 records the disjunction that [1] must join away. That difference is not a liberty we take with their rules but the content of Corollary 2, and it is what the quantale was introduced to express. The one place where a choice remains is the guard factor of WHILE (Remark 4), and it is forced by soundness rather than by fidelity. None of this constrains Section VII: Proposition 7 names no rule at all, and Theorem 7 quantifies over every renaming-stable rule set, so a reader who prefers different rules inherits the same ceiling.

The dichotomy is not a blanket verdict on disjunctive enforcement. It separates three uses of a walled source, and the separation is the practical content of the paper. Take 

σ(s) = σ(s1) = σ(s2) = S, the wall of Example 2, and a policy asking that the observation stay within S.

One source, one use. A report reads s once. The universal type of the output is ˆs and both bounds are S, so the policy is certified by any mechanism in the field. Nothing in this paper is needed, and nothing in this paper takes it away. 

One source, two uses. Example 1. The universal type is 

ˆs ⊗ ˆs = ˆ s and the set bound is S, which is the truth, while the multiset bound is S ⊗ S = ⊤. The program satisfies the policy and only the deferred read-out certifies it (Theorem 8). 

Two sources, one use each. A report reads s1 and s2, two values each separately within the wall. Here the universal type is ˆs1 ⊗ ˆs2 = ↓{ s1, s 2} and both bounds are S⊗S = ⊤, and that is correct: the two mediators may honour different disjuncts, one revealing r1 and the other r2, so the pair reveals the secret and the wall is genuinely breached. A programmer who knows the second pattern is being rejected cannot rewrite around it. Introducing a temporary, 

x := s; a := x; b := x, leaves the M -system exactly where it was: soundness forces Γ′(a) and Γ′(b) to contain every member of S, and the read-out is their tensor, so Proposition 7 applies verbatim. The obstruction lives in the type domain rather than in the program text, which is why the response has to be a different object rather than a different coding style. The third pattern is the one the tensor was introduced for, and the set object does not weaken it. What the set object adds is the ability to tell the second pattern from the third, which the multiset object cannot: it sees ↓(2 es) and ↓(es1 +es2 )

and specialises both to ⊤. Reading a walled value twice and reading two walled values are different events, and the distinction survives exactly one step of specialisation, the step that Theorem 7 shows a renaming-capable mechanism cannot take. X. R ELATED WORK 

Flow-sensitive types and principal typings. Lattice-based information flow begins with certification [7] over Denning’s lattice model [6] and its type-theoretic formulation [8]; see [9] for the classical survey, and [10] for dependency analysis of the kind this paper types. Principal typings in general are surveyed in [11]. The flow-sensitive family, its universal object and internal completeness are from [1], with polynomial-time inference of the principal typing in [12], and the relation to Hoare-style independence logic [18] is established there. Dynamic policies received a principal treatment in the same style [19], which isolates policy-independent inference as the feature worth preserving, and that is the feature Section VIII-A shows survives the repair. All of this assumes the combination operator is a join, which is the hypothesis removed here. 

Quantale of information. The lattice of information [5] has semantic refinements such as the demonic lattice [14] and, on the analysis side, per-based models of dependency [13]. The quantale’s semantic model, the composition lemmas and the ethical-wall examples are from [2], which leaves the type system layer open. Figure 1 is anchored to their lemmas rule by rule. Their abstract domain map is, in our notation, the specialisation ασ0 at the all-principal labelling, so the positive side of Theorem 6 has in effect been instantiated once by the authors, while the disjunctive side has not been approached. Their closing section also asks for relational analyses and suggests path sensitivity as a route, and Remark 8 places our results with respect to that programme. 

Disjunctive enforcement. Disjunctive policies have been enforced for a database query language against a purpose-built security condition [3]. The homomorphism from queries into the quantale used there is a single concrete map rather than a family indexed by labellings, and principality, specialisation and completeness are not addressed. By Theorem 7 any such mechanism that supports renaming is subject to the same ceiling. 

Quantales. Free quantales and the downset construction with Day convolution are standard [17] and are used as given. The contribution is that the side condition of the universal property is the dichotomy condition of the type system. 

Abstract interpretation. Completeness of abstract interpreta-tions is a classical subject [16], and completeness of abstract domains for dependency in particular has been studied through abstract non-interference [20]. The question there is which abstractions are complete for a fixed semantics. Here it is which universal object an entire family of type systems specialises from, and the answer is a pair of objects rather than one. Disjunctive completion and the powerset operator on abstractions [21], [22] are the classical counterpart of the step from L to U; Remark 6 explains why the step only pays once the concrete side is a quantale. XI. C ONCLUSION 

Disjunctive policies were given a semantics before they were given an enforcement mechanism, and building the mechanism turns out to force a choice that the lattice era never had to make. The universal object that made flow-sensitive typing reusable across policies splits: the free commutative quantale on the variables keeps the entire mechanism and loses same-source correlation, the free object with idempotent generators keeps precision and loses renaming, and the lattice-era object loses branch disjunction. No object with a renaming-capable specialisation improves on the first, so for the ethical-wall and secret-sharing labels a second read of a source leaves nothing to certify, and programs that respect the wall are rejected. Deferring specialisation to the judgement level recovers the missing precision, through the least sound join-preserving read-out. Two directions follow. The characterisation of idempotents, and with it the reading of the dichotomy as classical against disjunctive labels, is stated over a finite set of secrets, and the infinite case is open. And the relational hierarchy that [2, §VIII] calls for begins where Section VIII stops: keeping shared sources visible until the read-out recovers the correlation between two uses of one variable, while correlating two uses of one branch needs path sensitivity and a mechanism outside the shape studied here.
